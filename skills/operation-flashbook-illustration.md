An illustration is a literal picture, made to be rendered as a picture: inline SVG or an image, never a chart with labels. It is elaborate and artistically attractive, not straight lines and boxes: curved paths, gradients, layered shapes, organic forms. It carries the page's meaning through imagery drawn from the flashbook's own prose and nothing from outside its subject.

Simple yet complex: one thing, not trying to cover too much, drawn so it rewards looking. Words inside it are a title or a few labels at most, never below 16px on a 360px-wide screen, legible in both themes.

A flowchart is hand-written SVG drawn for a phone held upright: it runs top to bottom, at most two shapes side by side, its viewBox taller than wide, filling the page width. Before publishing, it is rendered at 360 by 740 in a headless browser and every text box is measured to sit inside its shape and inside the viewBox; the measurement takes no screenshot.

An illustration may be a flowchart, and then the two are one: the flowchart itself is illustrated, its connections given a style and a feeling, a fat bright red hand-coloured arrow or whatever the page's meaning asks, with the things around it drawn in. A feeling description is as good as a visual one. Two bare charts never follow each other. Image, then text, then image again.
